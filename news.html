Product Launch — Sikaflow

Sikaflow Goes Nationwide: Ghana's All-in-One Business Platform for MSMEs Now Live

15 July 20264 min readSource: Graphic Online

Sikaflow, the business management platform developed by Trident Aliska, launched nationwide as a free Android app and on smart POS terminals shipping to merchants across all sixteen regions. It brings together bookkeeping, invoicing, inventory, sales tracking, expense management, and digital reporting in one platform aimed at traders, workshops, farms, and small offices alike.

The platform also supports offline use and USSD access for basic phones, syncing automatically once connectivity returns, and includes an AI assistant offering conversational support in Twi, Ga, Hausa, and Ewe alongside English. Beyond day-to-day operations, Sikaflow generates clean, IFRS-for-MSMEs-compliant records that partner banks can use to turn loan assessments that once took months into a matter of minutes.

Trident Aliska CEO Aleem Kumi described the launch as more than a software release: "Sikaflow is more than technology it is an inclusive economic empowerment platform." The launch follows a six-year research programme into the support small and medium enterprises need to grow, and the platform is now available at sikaflow.com.

Joint Venture Launch

Trident Digital Tech Holdings and Aliska Business Advisory launch joint venture to accelerate Ghana's Digital Transformation

31 July 20265 min readSource: Graphic Online

Trident Digital Tech Holdings Ltd. (Nasdaq: TDTH), a Singapore-headquartered digital technology company, has entered a 50/50 joint venture with Aliska Business Advisory and Research Limited, a leading Accra-based advisory and research firm. The resulting entity, Trident Aliska Digital Tech Ghana Ltd., will develop and commercialise digital technology solutions for public and private sector clients across Ghana and the wider continent.

Under the agreement, Trident Aliska takes the lead on technology development, platform engineering, and technical system management, with an emphasis on sovereign technology, cybersecurity, and data protection. Both partners will jointly oversee system maintenance, AI and machine-learning upgrades, and the operational training needed to support nationwide rollout. The deal leans on Aliska's standing in financial advisory, research and data analytics, accounting, and regulatory compliance, alongside Trident's technology base — including its Tridentity digital identity platform — to serve Ghana's fast-growing digital economy under initiatives like the Digital Ghana Agenda.

Trident Aliska CEO and Aliska founder Aleem Kumi framed the venture's purpose plainly: "We do not just build technology; we architect the conditions for African prosperity." Trident's Founder and CEO Soon Huat Lim added that the partnership is designed to unlock synergies between Trident's technology capabilities and Aliska's local expertise, with further details on the venture's initial project pipeline expected in the coming weeks.

Sikaflow's Ghana Launch Confronts MSME Formalization Headwinds

23 June 20265 min readSource: AU-Startups

Trident Digital Tech Holdings rolls out a comprehensive digital platform in Ghana, aiming to formalize over 2 million MSMEs, but faces a market fraught with existing digital solutions and infrastructure gaps.

The promise of Sikaflow lies in its integrated approach. It bundles digital commerce, business management, tax automation, and financial record creation into a single platform accessible via Android, iOS, web, POS terminals, and even USSD channels. This multi-channel access, including offline capabilities, is a direct nod to the varied connectivity realities across Ghana. The core value proposition is clear: transforming daily business activities into verifiable digital financial records, which can then unlock access to credit and other financial services for businesses that have historically operated without formal documentation.

The Landscape of Formalization

Ghana's fintech landscape is one of the most active in West Africa, with an estimated 200 firms operating across payments, lending, insurtech, and regtech. Companies like Expresspay, Zeepay, Nsano, and Hubtel have already made significant inroads in facilitating digital transactions and mobile money, which over 80% of Ghanaian adults use. The challenge isn't a lack of digital tools, but rather the persistent hurdles of technology adoption costs, inadequate infrastructure, and complex regulatory processes that discourage formalization for many MSMEs.
